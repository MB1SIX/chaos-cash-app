# YOUR RIGHTS WHEN COPS SHOW UP

**Educational information only. Not legal advice. Laws vary by state. Consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.**

---

## The Short Version

You have rights. Cops have a job. Those two things are in a knife fight every single time, and the knife fight only goes one way if you know the rules.

## When They Knock

- You do not have to open the door. Not ever. Not for "just a quick question."
- Ask through the door: "Do you have a warrant?" If yes, ask them to slide it under. Read it. If it doesn't name you or your address, it doesn't apply to you.
- If no warrant, say: "I do not consent to any search." Then shut up. Silence is not consent, but "go ahead" is.

## During Questioning

- "Am I free to leave?" If yes, leave. If no, you're being detained.
- "I want a lawyer." Say it once, clearly. Then say nothing else. Not "but I didn't do anything." Not "can I just explain." Nothing.
- You can refuse a breathalyzer or field sobriety test in most states without it being a crime — but refusing often means automatic license suspension. Know your state's implied consent law before you decide.
- You do not have to consent to a phone search. Ever. They need a warrant for that.

## What to Never Say

- Your name, if you can avoid it in some states — but most require it during a lawful stop.
- Where you were, who you were with, what you were doing.
- "I was just holding it for someone." That's an admission.
- Anything while you're high, drunk, or scared. Fear makes you confess to shit you didn't do.

## The One Rule That Saves Lives

Polite, firm, silent. "Officer, I'm exercising my right to remain silent and I want an attorney." Repeat as needed. Do not argue. Do not explain. Do not perform.
