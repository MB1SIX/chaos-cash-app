# COURT PREP CHECKLIST

**Educational information only. Not legal advice. Laws vary by state. Consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.**

---

## Before the Date

- Confirm the date, time, and courtroom number. Write it down. Don't trust your memory.
- Dress like you're going to a funeral you respect. Collared shirt, slacks, closed shoes. No hats, no shorts, no flip-flops, no tank tops. The judge decides your fate and judges are vain.
- Print three copies of every document: your ID, any paperwork from the court, proof of insurance or registration if it's a traffic thing.
- If you have a lawyer, confirm they're showing up. If you don't, decide whether you need one before you walk in — not after.

## What to Bring

- Government ID.
- Cash or card for any fines.
- A notebook and pen. Write down everything the judge says.
- Your phone, charged, silenced. You'll want to look up terms mid-hearing and you can't do that if it's dead.
- A friend or family member if allowed — some courtrooms ban spectators.

## In the Courtroom

- Arrive thirty minutes early. Late is a warrant.
- Stand when the judge enters. Sit when told. Stand when you speak.
- Address the judge as "Your Honor." Not "judge," not "sir," not "hey."
- When it's your turn, speak clearly, answer only the question asked, and stop talking. Rambling is how you dig your own grave.
- If you don't understand something, say "I don't understand, Your Honor." Asking is not weakness. Guessing is.

## After

- Get everything in writing. Verbal promises from a judge mean nothing.
- If you were fined, ask for a payment plan before you leave. Don't agree to something you can't pay.
- If you lost, ask about appeal deadlines. Most are short — days, not weeks.
